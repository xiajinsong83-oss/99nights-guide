## What the long-term run shows

Surviving 99 nights proves you learned the loop; surviving for hundreds of days proves you automated it. This guide is built for that second stage. It shows a base designed around four pieces: strong layered walls, a defended entrance instead of an open gate, traps along the approach, and the Auto Food Generator handling hunger so you stop micromanaging stew at night. The video spends real time on the base tour — which wall segments take the Ram charge, where the traps sit, why the entrance funnels raids into a kill zone. That base logic is the real subject. Hundreds of days in, random encounters are not the problem anymore; a sloppy base design is, and it only reveals itself when a normal night snowballs.

## Takeaways you can use

- **The entrance is the weak point, not the walls.** A wall all around does nothing if the gate is undefended. The video's funnel-and-kill-zone design is what actually buys you time on raid nights.
- **Traps handle the boring half of the fight.** Once trap coverage is laid out, nights stop requiring you to chase every raider personally. That frees your attention for the big threats.
- **Automate food before walls.** The Auto Food Generator removes the most consistent night-time task. If you are still cooking manually at night, that is the first upgrade to queue.
- **Keep repair wood stocked.** Walls get smashed, especially by Ram charges. A long-term base is only as strong as the pile of wood sitting ready to patch it.

For players who beat 99 nights and want the run to keep going instead of restarting. If you are still on your first attempt, the early-game route is a better fit. This video optimizes the endgame loop, and its lessons only make sense once your base is already standing. The trap-and-automation focus is the real shift: early nights are solved by being active, hundreds of days are solved by making nights resolve without you. If your current base still needs you present every night, you are not yet at the stage this guide assumes.
