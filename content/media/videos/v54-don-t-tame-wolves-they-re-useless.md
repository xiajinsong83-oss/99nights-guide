## What this video actually shows

Every taming guide in the game makes wolves look like the obvious first companion — they are common, they look fierce, and you meet them early. This video argues the opposite: wolf companions fail in real survival, and the run shows why. The footage follows tamed wolves through the situations where a companion is supposed to pull weight — night defense at the bonfire, a dangerous biome trip, a cultist raid — and points out exactly where a wolf underperforms. Then the video pivots: what you should tame instead, given the same recipe effort.

The strongest evidence is the comparison montage: a wolf companion shadowing the run for an entire night while The Deer patrols, and doing exactly nothing when the cultists breach the wall. The constructive half then lists the companions that actually pull weight — the ones that fight at your side during the hours you built them for. The verdict is not "never tame anything early"; it is "do not spend your first good recipe on the animal that looks coolest."

## Key takeaways

- **The wolf fails where you need it most.** Watch the night-defense footage — a companion that does nothing during the dangerous hours is just food expense.
- **Recipe effort competes with better tames.** Every snack stack spent on a wolf is effort not spent on a useful companion; the video quantifies the tradeoff by showing the alternatives.
- **Appearance is not performance.** Wolves look like guard dogs; the run demonstrates what they actually do under pressure.
- **"What to tame instead" is the useful half.** The video's constructive list is where the real value sits — the tames that actually carry a survival run.
