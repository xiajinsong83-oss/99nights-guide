## What the fortress test shows

The Deer patrols your campfire at night and punishes anyone caught in the dark, so the obvious answer is walls. AJBLOX takes that idea seriously and builds a triple-wall fortress to test it. The video is structured as an experiment, not a walkthrough: first the build (three concentric wall layers, with gaps designed to slow the Deer down instead of losing you), then the night when it arrives. You see whether three layers hold where one or two fail, and how the Deer interacts with the wall gaps. The bonus segment — the polar bear revenge — is the consequence of spending that much time away from camp with heavy materials on the way home, and it is a good reminder that base design and travel safety are linked.

## Takeaways you can use

- **Layered walls absorb time, not just damage.** The point of three walls is not that they are unbreakable. It is that the Deer spends night after night chewing through them while you stay alive.
- **Gap design matters.** Solid walls with no gaps trap you inside with it. The fortress keeps controlled gaps so you can move and it funnels the raiding path.
- **Three walls are expensive.** This is a late-game build. Getting the materials to lay three concentric rings takes a base that already earns its keep.
- **Travel home with a big load is risky.** The polar bear segment is the lesson: don't carry your fortress materials through open terrain at dusk.

## Who should watch

For players whose walls keep failing against the Deer and want to see what "way too many walls" looks like in practice. It is a spectacle as much as a guide. If you just need one solid wall to survive, start with the text base guide instead — the fortress is the endgame answer. The build's real lesson is the gap design. Three walls with no gaps trap you inside with the Deer; three walls with controlled gaps let it chew through time while you stay safe. Copy the gap logic, not just the wall count.
