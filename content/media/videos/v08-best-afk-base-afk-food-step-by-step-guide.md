## What the AFK build covers

An AFK base sounds like a lazy shortcut, but this video is the opposite: it is a full engineering run through making your camp survive while you are not babysitting it. Two systems get built. The first is the base layout — walls, entrance, and trap placement arranged so nights resolve without you. The second is AFK food, centered on Crock Pot stew automation, with the actual resource math shown: how much input feeds how long, and where the overflow goes. Then the video stresses it: a 200-day AFK test where the camp is left running and you see what breaks when nobody is watching. That stress test is the honest part — it shows which parts of the build hold and which quietly fail over time.

## Takeaways you can use

- **AFK means automated food first.** A base you can ignore is only possible because stew keeps the hunger bar steady. If your food loop is manual, nothing else about going AFK works.
- **Watch the resource math, not just the layout.** The video numbers out the input flow; copying the base shape without matching the supply chain leaves you starving on day 40.
- **The 200-day test shows the failure points.** Long AFK reveals which wall segments and trap spots degrade fastest. Build the stress-test fixes in from the start.
- **AFK does not mean unguarded.** Raiders still come. The base resolves nights on its own, but only because the funnel and traps were built before you ever stepped away.

For players who want overnight progress without staying logged in. The step-by-step math makes it actually copyable, which is rarer than it sounds — most AFK videos just show the result. If you are still dying on active nights, fix those first; an AFK base only protects a camp that already survives. The Crock Pot stew automation is the part people copy wrong.
