## What the hide-spot method shows

Most raid advice is about building bigger. This video is about hiding better, and it works. The spot is a small setup: a tree plus a planted sapling that together create a position the raiders cannot reach or target properly. From there, you pick raiders off at no damage instead of trading blows at the campfire. The footage shows the setup being built (exactly which tree, how the sapling is placed) and then a full raid from inside the spot — raiders file in, line up, and die without ever landing a hit on you. It is the opposite extreme of the maze-base approach: near-zero cost, near-zero risk, as long as your campfire itself is not the thing being attacked.

## Takeaways you can use

- **The sapling placement is the whole trick.** Any tree won't do — the video shows the geometry that blocks the raiders' pathing. Copy the spot relative to the tree, not just "stand near wood."
- **You still kill, you don't just hide.** The method works because you shoot from cover. Ravers left alive keep doing damage to your walls and camp.
- **Cheap, but not infinite.** This is a starter raid solution. If raids scale up in numbers or frequency, a hide spot alone stops being enough, and the maze-base logic starts making sense.
- **Your campfire still needs defense.** The hide spot protects you; it doesn't protect the base. Walls matter even when you are tucked in a tree.

## Who should watch

For newer players whose walls cannot handle the first cultist raids yet. The method gets you through a raid night with almost no resources spent, and the video makes the setup easy to copy. Once you survive a few raids this way, decide whether the upgrade path leads to walls or a maze. The tree-and-sapling trick is cheap because it is a bandage — it buys you nights, not a base. Don't get comfortable with it. As raid numbers grow, a hide spot stops covering you, and you will need to graduate to real perimeter defense. Watch the video, use the method, and plan the exit from it.
