A good base in 99 Nights in the Forest is not a fortress, it is a ring. The center of the ring is the campfire, the ring itself is one layer of walls, the lanes between ring and camp hold your traps, and everything you need mid-fight sits inside the glow. Players who build sprawling compounds lose because they spread the fire thin. Small rings hold; big rings leak.

## The standard layout, in order

1. **Campfire core.** Every wall, crate and trap goes inside the fire's light radius. If your builder places a wall in the dark, move it. The light radius is the arena, and outside the arena nothing you build matters.
2. **Wall ring.** One layer around the fire early, two layers once the Ram starts charging. Leave one or two intentional gaps that you cover with traps — open shooting lanes beat closed boxes, because you need to poke enemies with the spear.
3. **Trap lanes.** Enemies path toward the fire along predictable routes. Lay traps on those routes, not evenly across the grass. A spent trap is a hole in your wall, so re-arm every morning.
4. **Storage inside.** Shelves and crates sit next to the fire, not out by the fence. Mid-siege, whoever restocks runs ten steps, not a sprint across open ground.

## Walls: what they stop and what they do not

Walls slow the Deer's minions and make cultist raids walk into your traps. They do not stop the Ram, which charges through wood like it is not there. Three practical consequences:

- **Keep a repair stack of wood** in a crate reachable from inside the ring. Repairing mid-raid is faster than rebuilding the next morning.
- **Reinforce the fence sections the Ram likes.** Watch which lane it charges on the first night it shows up — it picks a lane and commits to it.
- **Do not rely on a single gap.** One gap is a shooting lane; three gaps are three breach points.

Later in the game, the community has done wilder versions: the triple-wall fortress tests against the Deer, and the kill-funnel maze base that survives 500-plus days of cultist raids. Both are end-game base design, and both assume the ring already works. Nail the ring first.

## Traps, not walling every meter

Lining every meter with walls costs more wood than an early run has, and it hides exactly the lanes your traps need. The ratio that works is: **walls where enemies would walk, traps where they would die**. Put traps on the approach lanes, leave the ring gaps you control, and re-arm each morning. Early traps mostly stall; later bench unlocks handle heavier enemies, which is the same "bench before hoarding" lesson wearing different clothes.

## Shelves, crates and the food separation

Shelves and crates are cheap storage, and cheap storage is how you keep a siege organized:

- **Food in one crate, upgrade wood in another.** When builders raid the food crate for their next wall, the squad starves mid-fight. One crate per job.
- **Keep several meals of buffer per player.** Hunger always bites during a siege, never at dinner.
- **Stack the repair wood at a single marked spot** so nobody has to remember where it is at 2 AM.

## The night-watch rotation

This is the detail that separates bases that survive from bases that look fine and then vanish:

- **One player guards, the rest restock.** Everyone fighting at once means the unguarded gap gets used the second something slips through.
- **Assign lanes before dusk** — east wall, trap lane, fire duty. "We'll figure it out at night" is how a cultist walks in.
- **Call Ram charges early.** The second you hear the charge line, the builder on repair duty moves; the guard keeps the pressure off.

## Where the maze base comes in

Once the ring holds on its own, the community's endgame answer is the kill-funnel maze: walls arranged so raiders walk a long, trap-lined corridor toward the fire instead of taking the shortest path. The video runs that survive 500-plus days of cultist raids all use some version of this. It is not a night-10 build — it needs the wood economy of a settled base — but the principle (funnel enemies where your traps wait) applies from the moment you lay the first ring.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Building half the base outside the light radius | Darkness negates the walls you built | Keep everything inside the glow |
| One giant perimeter ring | Long walls = many leak points | Two small rings beat one huge one |
| No repair crate inside the base | Ram + empty hands = rebuild by torchlight | Wood crate at a fixed inner spot |
| Everyone fighting at night | The unguarded gap gets used | Guard/restock rotation |
| Traps spread everywhere | Wood wasted, real lanes unprotected | Traps on the attack paths |
