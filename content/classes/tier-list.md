There is no single best class in 99 Nights in the Forest. There is a best class for your situation, and this tier list is our read after the September 5 new-class drop and the September 19 Class Shop Revamp. Two things changed the board recently: Woodsman, Escape Artist and Elemental joined the roster, and the shop now sells every class permanently instead of rotating stock, so "wait until it appears" stopped being a strategy.

{{< notice title="Opinion, not gospel" >}}Balance patches move classes between tiers. The reasoning below matters more than the letter grade, and prices/perks are always worth confirming in the current shop.{{< /notice >}}

## S tier: the picks that justify their price

- **Medic.** Healing and revives are the difference between a wiped squad and a squad that walks off a bad night. Every serious run wants one, and hard mode forgives a lot of mistakes if a Medic is on watch.
- **Lumberjack.** The wood economy runs the whole base. Fast chopping compounds into faster campfire and bench upgrades, which compounds again into better tools. It is the snowball class, and the snowball never stops rolling.

## A tier: strong and flexible

- **Woodsman.** Arrived September 5 with the unique Woodsman's Axe, log-storage and giant-sapling perks. It is a second wood engine, and it pairs with Lumberjack so well that a squad running both races through its base phase. Its quest — chopping 40 trees in 30 seconds — is also the one we hear the most complaining about.
- **Scavenger.** Keeps supplies flowing. The support role you only miss when the food crate is empty.
- **Cook.** Food pressure is real on long runs; less impressive on short sessions, essential on 99-night marathons.

## B tier: situational

- **Ranger.** Shines during exploration and rescue phases, ordinary on pure defense nights.
- **Assassin.** Great in skilled hands, rough for new players. The burst damage is real, and so is the paper-thin margin for error.
- **Escape Artist.** The September 5 addition with sprinting, chase-escape and detection perks. It keeps you alive when you refuse to fight, which is a legitimate solo playstyle. Its quest — being chased by seven Alpha Wolves at once — is a fantastic stress test of whether the click matches the ambition.
- **Elemental.** The other September 5 addition: biome helmet, status effects, speed and night vision. Strong when the right biome rolls, forgettable otherwise.

## C tier: the learning tool

- **Camper.** Free and perfectly balanced, which is the problem: once you know the loop, every other class does a job Camper does not. Keep it for your first run, then graduate.

## Pick by situation

| Situation | Best pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Brand new player | Camper | Free, balanced, nothing to learn |
| Small squad of 2–3 | Lumberjack + Medic | Wood engine plus sustain |
| Full squad of 4+ | Lumberjack, Medic, Scavenger, fill role | Economy and healing covered |
| Solo run | Scavenger or Escape Artist | Self-sufficient supply, or built to run |
| Rescue sprint | Ranger | Map coverage wins the rescue chain |
| Forest Rage hard mode | Medic + Assassin | Survival plus burst damage |
| Big base projects | Lumberjack + Woodsman | Double wood engine |
| Biome-heavy exploration | Elemental | Status effects per biome |

## Why the top two never move

Medic and Lumberjack have sat at the top through every balance patch because they are not power picks, they are *system* picks. Healing and revives are the only thing that turns a failed night into a recovered one, and fast chopping is the only input that makes every upgrade arrive sooner. The Class Shop Revamp changed *how* you buy classes — permanent stock instead of rotation — not which ones matter.

## The trap of buying Assassin first

New players eye Assassin for the burst damage and almost always regret it. High damage on a glass body punishes the exact mistakes a new player makes: standing in the open, overextending, missing the kite. It climbs the list only once you already know how to survive a night. Buy it as your second or third class, not your first.

## How the September updates moved the board

The September 5 new-class drop pushed Woodsman, Escape Artist and Elemental up the board before anyone had them on a tier list. Woodsman pairs with Lumberjack as a double wood engine, Escape Artist is a legit solo survival pick, and Elemental sits where biome rolls matter. The September 19 shop revamp matters less to *who* is good and more to *when* you can buy — permanent stock means you no longer wait on rotation for the class you want.

## The one rule

Your squad should never field zero Medics. Healing decides night outcomes more than any weapon, any trap and any wall section combined. Everything else in this list is negotiable.

Woodsman is the sleeper pick from the September drop. It pairs with Lumberjack as a second wood engine, and on a big-base project run that double income cuts the bench timeline by a full day. Escape Artist is the solo pick that finally makes a one-player run viable without leaning on the tamed bear. Elemental is niche but real in biome-heavy weeks, where status effects line up with the terrain.
