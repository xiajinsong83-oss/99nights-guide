Modifier Flames are the reason a mid-game loadout feels nothing like an early one. A Flame attaches to a weapon or tool and changes how it behaves: damage type, status effects, or the resources it yields. A mid-tier weapon with the right Flame attached beats a plain top-tier weapon without one, and in [Forest Rage hard mode]({{< relref "/advanced/forest-rage-hard-mode" >}}) that difference is the whole run.

## What Flames actually do

- **Modify the item.** Attach a Flame and the weapon or tool gains the associated behavior — a damage type, an effect, or a yield bonus depending on the Flame.
- **Stack with gear tiers.** The Flame does not replace your weapon's own power; it multiplies what the weapon already does. Upgraded weapon plus attached Flame is the line between "fine" and "broken."
- **Come random.** Code rewards hand out *random* Flames — `awardwinner_yay` gives three (Frog, Thanksgiving, Fairy in the current rotation), `forestwakesup26` gives three more (Frog, Fairy, Pet). Collection is part of the loop.

## How to get Flames

- **Codes.** Both live Diamond codes above are really Flame deliveries with Diamonds attached. Claim them on the [Codes page]({{< relref "/codes" >}}).
- **Gameplay drops.** Late-night kills and event content drop Flames as you go.
- **The Sketchy Salesman.** This is the system the April 18 Offerings update built around: a nighttime merchant who drifts near your camp and rolls random **Fire Offerings** — nine different offering effects with their own purchase rules. It is how the old Modifier Flames page became a Fire Offerings guide, and it is the steady supply line once you are mid-run.

## How to attach them

1. Open your inventory or equipment menu.
2. Select the weapon or tool you want to modify.
3. Attach the Flame. Some Flames only fit certain item types, so check the compatibility note.
4. Confirm — the item's stats update immediately.

## How not to waste them

- **Do not burn a good Flame on a starter tool.** Save the attachments for mid-tier or better gear. A Flame on a basic axe is a Flame wasted.
- **Match the Flame to the role.** Combat classes want damage Flames; Lumberjacks and Scavengers want resource-yield Flames. Putting a damage Flame on a gathering tool is a common, expensive mistake.
- **Start collecting early.** Flames are a hard-mode prerequisite. If you reach Forest Rage with zero Flames banked, the prep checklist already failed.

## Why the Sketchy Salesman matters

The Fire Offerings merchant appears at night near camp and rolls random offerings. Because the pool has nine effects with purchase rules, what he offers tonight may be useless and tomorrow may be exactly what your loadout needed. Treat him as a steady, repeatable supply rather than a one-stop shop: visit him, buy the offerings that fit, and come back next night.

## How to actually collect Flames

The steady supply lines are the Sketchy Salesman at night, code rewards, and gameplay drops. The Salesman rolls random Fire Offerings near camp, so visit him, buy what fits, and come back tomorrow. The codes are the fastest burst: `awardwinner_yay` and `forestwakesup26` each hand you three Flames, which is most of a loadout in one sitting. Do not burn them on starter gear — save the attachments for mid-tier or better weapons.

## The hard-mode math

In [Forest Rage hard mode]({{< relref "/advanced/forest-rage-hard-mode" >}}), Flames stop being a nice-to-have. Corruption and Rift fights last longer, and the difference between a weapon that barely kills and one that melts through is usually the Flame attached. Squads that reach hard mode without a Flame bank spend the first week fighting at half efficiency. That is why the codes that pay out three Flames each are really gear-upgrade coupons, not free cosmetics.

## Which Flame to attach first

When you only have one or two Flames, put the damage one on your main weapon and save any resource-yield one for a Lumberjack's axe. Do not split a single Flame across early gear — a focused upgrade on your best tool beats two half-upgrades on disposable ones.
