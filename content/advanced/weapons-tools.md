Your crafting bench is the whole economy of 99 Nights in the Forest. Everything you survive with — tools, weapons, armor, storage, later the map and compass — comes out of it, and its upgrade level gates what you are even allowed to build. This page is the tier path and the loadouts that make it worth it.

## What the bench tiers unlock

The bench unlocks recipe groups in order. Exact names and material costs shift with patches, so this is the progression shape, not a price table:

| Bench tier | What opens up |
|---|---|
| 1 | Basic axe, spear, simple storage |
| 2 | Better tools, traps, shelves |
| 3 | Weapons tier-up, basic armor |
| 4+ | Late-game weapons, advanced traps, map and compass |

The June 13 Forest Wakes Up update reworked tool bench upgrading specifically, and the March 21 Crafting Update added the Recycler, Flag and Log Gate on top — so the mid-game bench has real decisions, not just a straight upgrade line.

## The three tools that run the game

- **Axe.** Wood gathering, and upgrading it is the single biggest economy multiplier in the game. Every other upgrade gets funded by a better axe.
- **Spear.** The reliable early weapon, and it stays relevant because it reaches enemies from behind your walls. No ammo, no risk, poke from the glow.
- **Map and Compass.** Unlocks the exploration loop that leads to the missing-children rescues. Late game, this is not optional.

## Weapons by phase

| Phase | Best weapon | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Spear | Safe from behind walls |
| Mid | Upgraded spear or axe | Damage catches up to the threats |
| Late | Top-tier bench weapons | Bears and hard mode demand burst |

One number worth internalizing: community rifle testing counts the shots each animal actually takes to drop — a weak rifle eats most of a magazine on a wolf, and a whole lot more on a bear. That is the argument for not forcing fights before your weapons catch up. The Strong Axe hit-count video tells the same story on the melee side.

## The bench timeline matters

The order you unlock recipes changes the whole run. Bench level 2 is the inflection point: better tools, traps and shelves turn the economy from hand-to-mouth into surplus. Skipping it to hoard wood is the classic early mistake, because the surplus never arrives without the tools to produce it. The March 21 Crafting Update added the Recycler, Flag and Log Gate as mid-bench options, so there are real choices to make once the bench hits its stride.

## Loadout splits

- **Defense night:** spear or ranged weapon, repair stack of wood, light source. You are holding the ring, not winning a duel.
- **Gathering day:** axe, food, light armor. You should move fast and not look like a raiding party.
- **Rescue run:** weapon plus map and compass, plus a partner. Rescue routes end in guardian fights, and going alone is how the rescue fails.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hoarding wood before bench level 2 | Tools that never come | Bench first, hoard after |
| Forcing a bear fight early | Most of a rifle magazine for a lesson | Kite with traps until weapons catch up |
| Skipping map and compass | Rescue chain never starts | Build them the moment the bench allows |
| Burning a Flame on a starter tool | Multiplier wasted | Wait for mid-tier gear |

## Why the spear never retires

Players keep trying to replace the spear with fancier weapons, and almost always regret it. It costs nothing, needs no ammo, and reaches enemies from behind your walls — which is exactly where you want to be fighting. Upgraded axes and rifles matter for bears and hard mode, but for holding the ring on a normal night, the spear is still the safest tool in the kit.

The bench timeline is the real economy. Bench level 2 is the inflection point: better tools, traps and shelves turn the run from hand-to-mouth into surplus. Skipping it to hoard wood is the classic mistake, because surplus never arrives without the tools to produce it. Build the bench, then the weapons tier-up, and only then start spending on cosmetics.

Map and compass come online once the bench allows. Build them the moment you can, because the rescue chain starts there. Do not burn a Flame on a starter tool. Save Flames for mid-tier gear.

The tool order that actually works: axe first, then pickaxe, then spear upgrade, then traps. Chopping drives every other upgrade, so a better axe pays for itself in a day. Traps come after the bench because they are force multipliers on a base you already have, not a substitute for wood income. Do not skip the pickaxe either; stone gates the mid-tier tools.
